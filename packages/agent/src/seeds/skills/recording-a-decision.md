A decision records a choice that later work has to respect, and why it was made: what was chosen, what was turned down, and what follows from it. Its title states the decision, and its body is the text beneath it.

WHEN A CHOICE DESERVES A DECISION: record one when a choice constrains work beyond the task it came up in, when it turned down an alternative someone would reasonably propose again, or when undoing it later would be costly. A detail of one task that nothing else depends on is not a decision: it belongs in that task.

LOOK FOR AN EXISTING DECISION FIRST: <!-- include: decision-find-first --> A decision already recorded on the subject is linked, or superseded when the choice has changed. It is not written a second time.

WRITE IT: the title is the decision itself, stated as what was decided ("Sessions are stored server-side"), not as a topic ("Session storage"). The body says the context, the choice, the alternatives turned down and why, and the consequences. <!-- include: decision-create -->

ITS STATUS: a new decision is `proposed`. It becomes `accepted` when the user agrees to it, and that is theirs to say: ask, unless the user has already made the choice in this conversation. <!-- include: approval-ask-first --> <!-- include: decision-accept -->

LINK IT FROM WHAT IT CONSTRAINS: a decision reaches the work it governs through a link from that spec or task, not through a mention in its text. <!-- include: decision-link --> A decision linked from a spec governs every task of that spec, so link it from the spec when it constrains all of them and from a task when it constrains only that one.

SUPERSEDING AN OLDER DECISION: a decision that has changed is not edited into a different one. Write the new decision, link it to the one it replaces, and mark the old one `superseded`. <!-- include: decision-supersede --> A superseded decision is locked and cannot be moved back, so what was built under it still reads what it was built under.
